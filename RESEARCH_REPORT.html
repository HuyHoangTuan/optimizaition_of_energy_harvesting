Independent learning: progress and limits

Section 1: Why

Controlled independent DQN learned across the tested transmitter counts. Corrected recurrent learning worked, but its completed one-transmitter comparison did not establish an improvement.

The research asks whether secondary transmitters can learn separate energy-harvesting policies, and whether memory helps with delayed observations. A secondary user (SU) is a simulated secondary radio transmitter. Two primary users (PUs) share the model.

The original shared controller broadcast one action and learned summed reward. Increasing SU count therefore did not create separate learners. The new path gives each SU its own policy, target, optimizer, replay, random generator and reward. Corrected DRQN also gives each SU private memory.

The baseline was recovered retrospectively after the first independent-agent evaluation. The recovered source is a historical candidate; its connection to the original saved logs remains unproven. Exact reproduction of the paper or saved curves is unestablished.

These studies use different action sets, battery rules, traces and evaluation banks. Their sequence describes research progress; it cannot isolate one change as the cause of improvement. Model reward is not validated physical throughput.

Section 2: Summary

The contribution is a checked sequence: qualitative baseline recovery, a retained failed independent recipe, controlled DQN learning, and a mixed corrected DRQN comparison.

  • Baseline: recovered final policies beat their own random controls, although dynamic generalization varied across seeds.
  • Early independent DQN: all N = 1, 3, 5 group means stayed below random. Agent isolation did not guarantee useful learning.
  • Controlled DQN: all six tested groups passed the recorded learning, action-value health, score and execution gates.
  • Corrected DRQN: mean paired gain was +0.010206447 reward per SU per slot; only one of three seeds improved. The frozen criterion failed.
  • Scaling: multi-SU DRQN is implemented. N = 2 was not admitted by the runtime budget; N = 3 was skipped.

An episode has 20 slots. Baseline values below are reward per episode. Later tables divide reward by episodes × 20 × SU count. Recorded score counts executed transmissions; reward also includes branch penalties. Three training seeds remain three independent training replicates, even with 100 evaluation episodes per policy.

Fixed actions use ρ = 0.4. Dynamic actions use ρ ∈ {1/8,…,7/8}, excluding 0.4. Historical action sets have 34/238 fixed/dynamic choices, including zero power; later sets have 32/224.

Section 3: Changes

This pull request includes the earlier independent-agent and stability work, plus the corrected recurrent implementation and this report. The scientific files copied for the recurrent study remain byte-identical to the studied sources.

From isolated agents to controlled learning

The early evaluated recipe used 64 × 64 layers, Adam 0.001, Huber loss, batch 8 and executed-only battery debit. Its failed learning result led to controlled checks rather than immediate scaling.

The controlled study restored current-source traces and requested debit, which can charge failed transmit requests. The retained plain DQN uses 128 × 128 layers, SGD 0.003, MSE, batch 64 and γ = 0.99. Soft targets update with τ = 0.001 every fifth zero-based episode, including zero. No gradient clipping or Double DQN is used in the selected recipe.

Four one-factor probes and Double DQN did not meet the promotion rule. Double DQN still learned; its failed promotion is distinct from a failed learning gate.

Qθ(x) = W₃ ReLU(W₂ ReLU(W₁x + b₁) + b₂) + b₃

q = Qθ(x)[a]; y = r + γ(1 − d) maxa′ Qθ⁻(x′)[a′]

MSE loss = mean[(q − y)²]

Huber₁(e) = ½e² when |e| < 1; |e| − ½ otherwise, where e = q − y

Here θ is policy weights, θ⁻ target weights, a the selected action, r local reward, d the terminal flag, and x the learner input. A terminal target is reward only. Early learning uses Huber; the controlled and paired recipes use MSE.

Chronological recurrent replay

The legacy recurrent path used unrelated replay transitions as a sequence and discarded acting hidden state. Corrected GRU64 replay stores complete 20-transition episodes with 21 observations.

Both networks process four full episodes from zero state. Predictions use observations 0…19; successor targets use 1…20 while retaining the prefix from observation zero. Exactly 64 of 80 transition positions enter the loss. Acting memory advances during random decisions, refreshes after policy updates, and resets at episode boundaries.

qb,t = Qθ(ob,0, …, ob,t)[ab,t]

yb,t = rb,t + 0.99(1 − db,t) maxa Qθ⁻(ob,0, …, ob,t+1)[a]

M ⊂ {0,…,3} × {0,…,19}, |M| = 64 sampled without replacement

Loss = (1/64) Σ(b,t)∈M (qb,t − yb,t)²

Here b identifies an episode and M the sampled loss positions. Gradients retain complete within-episode prefixes. Evaluation uses separate hidden state without gradients and resets each episode. These corrections establish operational recurrence, not its performance advantage.

Here B is battery, H harvest, D debit, C requested cost, X executed transmission, k the transmit/harvest choice, p action power, μ = 1 − ρ, and T = 1. Primary power Pᵖ and channel grids g follow the source conventions. I is interference, Z the denominator and S the compatibility score.

Both action branches harvest; current harvest and debit affect the next boundary. Initial battery and observations are zero. Two PUs and 20 slots are used; v = 1 for the first ten slots, then zero. The eligibility threshold is checked per SU, not across aggregate SU interference.

Numeric dBm inside score arithmetic and density-grid channels prevent a physical-throughput claim. Historical equations differ and must not inherit these later formulas. Observations contain previous channels and further-lagged harvest; omitted current power and action history motivate, but do not prove, a memory benefit.

Included implementation references: DQN, corrected DRQN, joint environment, stability checks, and recurrent evaluation. Historical study inputs remain external.

Section 4: Result

The strongest completed result is controlled DQN learning. The recurrent result is mixed and does not support a claim of DRQN superiority.

Saved baseline late-training means were 26.473710 fixed and 42.831108 dynamic, measured over episodes 1501–1600. They are not held-out results. Recovery ran eight 1600-episode cells: three historical seeds per action mode and one current-source control per mode.

The full-precision mean gain was +0.010206447, with sample standard deviation 0.049089. One of three seeds improved. The frozen criterion required mean gain ≥ 0.05 and at least two positive seeds; neither condition passed.

All six final policies beat initial and random controls. Mean score was 0.835831 for DQN and 0.834370 for DRQN, so the reward gain was not a score gain. Three seeds do not establish statistical superiority.

The comparison matched trace banks, action maps and final checkpoint timing. It did not match capacity, replay, warmup, history, random-number consumption or compute. Its newly initialized DQN is the paired comparator; the earlier stability result cannot substitute for it.

DRQN charged 644.356997 training wall seconds versus 209.533602 for DQN across three full cells, approximately 3.08 times as much. This excludes overhead and does not measure inference speed, electrical energy or total elapsed time. N = 2 was denied by budget; N = 3 was skipped. Benchmarks are not learned-policy results.

Evidence and remaining limits

The corrected study records 37 scientific tests and independent scientific model review. A fresh rerun in this public checkout also passed all 37 tests with exit 0. This report introduces no new experiment. Focused document checks do not establish full independent browser acceptance, professor acceptance or physical validation.

The published source supports local tests. Full frozen-study reproduction also needs the exact external protocol and retained prior trace bundle described in DRQN.md. Those inputs, raw banks, checkpoints and archives are not included.

The next research question is when memory improves held-out performance under controlled information limits. The researcher must define the hypothesis, model interpretation, paired baselines, additional seeds and runtime budget before new training. Multi-SU DRQN remains future experimental work.
